Start

What this game is

AFWI is a two-player air and multi-domain wargame. One side is the United States. The other is the People's Republic of China. You fight over a short map of range bands, with aircraft, ships, drones, air defense, and cards for cyber, space, electronic warfare, and special operations.

You do not move a big stack of pieces every turn. On your turn you do a little, on purpose: play at most one enabler card, and either launch a squadron or do one move, one acquire, and one shot. The fight is about timing, sensors, and what your hidden mission actually scores.

This tutorial teaches the rules the app enforces. When you finish, go back and press Tutorial. That game is Campaign 2: Attrition, Standard, two ATO cycles, and a draft of 6 enabler cards.

Two ways the game ends. After the last air tasking order, the higher victory-point total wins. Or, the moment either side reaches Cyber Rate 4, that side wins immediately.
Check yourself

What are the two ways a game can end?

The side with more victory points after the last ATO wins. Reaching Cyber Rate 4 also wins on the spot, before points are compared.

Start

How a game is built

A game is a campaign. A campaign is one or more ATO cycles (air tasking orders). Campaign 1 is a single cycle. The tournament setup on the home screen is Campaign 2, which is two cycles. Campaign 3 runs five.

Each ATO cycle has the same spine:

  1. Draft. Each side picks a posture, then squadron cards, then enabler cards. You draft your whole roster, then the other side drafts theirs.
  2. Bid for initiative. Each side rolls a D4. The winner gets intel advantage, chooses who goes first, and gets one try to raise their cyber rate.
  3. Play intel. Each side peeks at some of the opponent's hidden cards. At least one enemy card stays hidden.
  4. Turns. You alternate until both sides pass on back-to-back turns. Any real action breaks the pass streak.
  5. Cleanup. Score the end-of-cycle mission terms, land whatever is still flying, and clear the board for the next cycle.

After the last cycle, hidden missions are revealed and the victory points are totaled.

Why both passes matter. If you pass and the opponent then activates a squadron, the cycle continues. The cycle ends only when a pass is answered with another pass.
Check yourself

What ends an ATO cycle?

Both players passing in succession. One pass is not enough. Taking any action, including playing only an enabler, resets the streak.

Start

The D4

Every roll in the game is a four-sided die. You need to meet or beat a number. A 4 is the best face. There is no six-sided die anywhere.

  • Normal. Roll one die.
  • Advantage. Roll two dice and keep the higher.
  • Disadvantage. Roll two dice and keep the lower.

Advantage and disadvantage cancel one for one, and they do not stack. One of each is a normal roll. Two advantages and one disadvantage is still a single advantage.

Some sensors add a flat bonus after the die is chosen. An AEW adds +2. Most radars add +1. The bonus is added to the kept die.

Hit rolls and damage rolls are different. Advantage and disadvantage change the to-hit roll. The exploding damage die is a plain single die. Missile defense is the exception: it puts disadvantage on both the hit roll and the damage roll.

In the app, click Roll when it is your die. Advantage shows two faces. The face that counts is highlighted.

Check yourself

You have advantage and the defender's missile defense gives you disadvantage. How many dice do you roll?

One. They cancel. You roll a normal single die for the hit. Missile defense still puts disadvantage on the damage roll if the attack has one.

The board

The map and range

The five center bands are the contested airspace, in a line from the US side to the PRC side. The US calls Band A "Band 1" and Band E "Band 5." The PRC counts the other way: Band E is their Band 1.

The US standoff, US airbase, and US contingency location all sit on Band A. They are one step from Band A. The PRC airbase and PRC standoff sit on Band E the same way. Range is the number of steps between two places. Adjacent places are range 1.

Click two locations. The second click is the destination.

Pick a start, then a destination.
Why a bomber's range is 6. US standoff to the PRC airbase is exactly 6 steps: standoff, A, B, C, D, E, PRC airbase. A B-52 or H-6K with surface range 6 can bomb the enemy airbase from its own standoff.
Check yourself

An F-22 moves 2. It is on Band A. Which center bands can it reach this turn?

Band B and Band C. Band B is 1 step away. Band C is 2. Band D is 3, so it is out of this move.

The board

Bases, standoff, and the CL

Airbase

Your airbase is home. Squadron cards deploy here face-down. It has three victory-point boxes. Each box the enemy fills is worth 1 VP, once, and the boxes last the whole campaign. Filling them does not shut the base down. Aircraft can still launch.

Standoff

Standoff is the deep rear. Only bombers and AEW may sit there. From standoff they shoot or sense across almost the whole map. An aircraft in standoff can be shot, and a hit on it lands on its squadron card.

Contingency location

Only the US has a contingency location. It is a dispersed site. You may deploy a squadron card there instead of the airbase. Activating a multi-aircraft squadron from the CL is risky: roll a D4, and only that many aircraft launch. The rest stay grounded on the card. A one-aircraft squadron (bomber, AEW, or ADA) always launches its single token. The CL has no VP boxes. A strike there can damage only one squadron card, and extra damage is lost.

Two ways to make the CL safe. The ACE posture, or the Flying Crew Chief enabler naming that squadron, launches every aircraft with no roll.
Check yourself

You put an F-16 squadron (4 aircraft) on the CL and roll a 2 when you activate it. What launches?

Two F-16s. The other two stay grounded on the card, where a base strike can kill them with the card. An F-16 on the airbase would have launched all four with no roll.

Your forces

Three kinds of things

Keep these separate. The app shows all three, and they are not the same object.

  • Squadron cards are the units you draft and later flip face-up. A card has two hits. When you activate it, it creates tokens.
  • Tokens are the pieces on the map. They move, acquire, and shoot. Fighters come four to a card. Recon drones come two. A bomber, AEW, or ADA card creates one token.
  • Enabler cards are the rest of the hand: ships, missiles, cyber, space, electronic warfare, special operations, recovery, and base defense. Some create tokens. Many are a one-time effect.

There is also a posture (how big a hand you draft) and a mission (how you score). Those are cards too, but you do not play them on a turn. You choose them during setup. The mission stays hidden until the game ends.

The B-52 is the odd squadron. The US has two B-52 squadron cards. Each one creates a single B-52. Every other squadron type has one card.
Check yourself

You activate the 90th Fighter Squadron, the F-22 card. How many tokens appear?

Four F-22 tokens, on Band A. A bomber card would have created one token, on Band A or in standoff.

Your forces

How to read a token

Every token has the same kinds of numbers. Ranges are in bands. Pick a unit and read it in plain language.

What the columns mean

  • Move is how many bands it may shift on the one move you get this turn. ADA moves 0.
  • Acquire is how far it can try to identify a face-down enemy, plus any bonus, and whether it rolls twice.
  • Air attack is range and the to-hit number against an acquired aircraft. A hit destroys that aircraft. There is no second damage roll.
  • Surface attack is range and the to-hit number against ships, bases, standoff aircraft, and ADA. A mark of ✸ means you then roll a D4 for how many hits to assign. Otherwise a hit is 1 point.
  • AV is the acquisition value, the roll an enemy needs to flip this token face-up. Higher is stealthier. F-22, F-35, and J-20 are AV 4.
  • Winchester is the ammo rule. Most aircraft go out of ammo unless the natural attack roll is a 4.
Check yourself

The F-16 air attack is "1 / 3" and the F-22 is "1 / 2". Which is the better air-to-air shot, and what does the F-16 have that the F-22 does not?

The F-22 hits on a 2 or better, so it is the better air-to-air shooter, and it is stealthier (AV 4). The F-16 needs a 3 or better in the air, but it also has a surface attack. The F-22 cannot bomb a base or a ship.

Your forces

How the pieces work together

Nothing on the board is good at every job. A turn is usually one piece finding, another piece killing, and a card changing the odds.

  • AEW is the finder. Range 3, +2, and it rolls twice. It does not shoot. Put it in standoff or on your front band and spend acquires with it.
  • Recon drones also roll twice to acquire, with a +1, and they never run out of ammo. They do not shoot.
  • Stealth fighters (F-22, F-35, J-20) are hard to acquire because their AV is 4. F-22 and J-20 only fight other aircraft. The F-35 can also strike the surface.
  • Older fighters (F-15, F-16, J-10, J-16, JH-7, J-15) are easier to see (AV 2) and come four to a card. Several of them can both dogfight and bomb.
  • Bombers (B-52, H-6K) reach across the map and roll exploding damage into a base. They are slow (move 1) and valuable to kill.
  • Attack drones bomb at a short range and a hard threshold (need a 3). They keep ammo on a 3 or 4, so they shoot more often than fighters.
  • ADA does not move. It shoots aircraft at its air range, and any attack that crosses its weapon zone is at disadvantage. You do not kill ADA with an air-to-air shot. You acquire it, then hit it with a surface attack, or assign a base-strike hit onto it after it has been found.
  • Ships move 1 band among the five center bands. They shoot air and surface a long way, the surface hit explodes, and they can spend an air salvo to defend a shot crossing their zone.
  • EC-130 does not shoot. A PRC attack launched from the band the EC-130 occupies is at disadvantage.
A normal US sequence. Activate AEW on one turn. Next turn, acquire a face-down token with the AEW, and if you already know a target, shoot it with a fighter in range. You cannot activate and shoot on the same turn.
Check yourself

You want to kill a face-down J-20 (AV 4) that is two bands away. Which of your pieces should attempt the acquire?

The AEW, if it can see that far. It rolls twice and adds 2, so a modest die still clears 4. An F-16 with no bonus needs a raw 4, and only if the J-20 is inside acquire range 2.

When you hold an enabler, four labels tell you how it lives in the campaign.

Words on strike cards are not decoration

  • A card that says it hits the airbase (HIMARS, Tomahawk, Sea Dragons, PLANMC) fills only the three VP boxes. It does not damage squadron cards.
  • A card that says it hits the base (ballistic, cruise, hypersonic) spreads damage across squadron cards and VP boxes, the way a bomb run does.
  • Unblockable cards ignore missile defense. PRC hypersonic, maritime strike cruise, Sea Dragons, and PLANMC are unblockable.
A cancel undoes the card. Red Horse, Resilient Bases, Defensive Cyber, Counter Space, and the decoy cards do not just skip a line in the log. They reverse the damage, the kill, the acquisition, or the cyber gain.

The full list of every enabler is in the lesson Enablers during play. You can filter it by name.

Check yourself

You play a single-use cyber card this cycle. Can you draft it again next cycle? What about a multi-use one you also played?

The single-use card is gone for the campaign. The multi-use card returns to your pool and can be drafted again.

Before the fight

Setup and the draft

Each side has its own deck of 54 cards. You never draft from a shared pool. The opponent cannot take your F-22 by drafting faster. Order inside your own draft does not deny you a later card.

In the app, one side finishes posture, squadrons, placement, and enablers. Then the other side does the same. You do not alternate pick by pick.

  1. Choose a posture the campaign allows.
  2. Choose squadron cards, at least one below the posture number and no more than the posture number. Place each face-down on the airbase, or for the US, on the CL.
  3. Choose enabler cards, up to the posture number. They go to your hidden hand.

You draft again at the start of every later ATO, from whatever you still own. A destroyed squadron stays destroyed. A spent single-use enabler stays spent. Surviving squadrons can be fielded again, but they come back missing any tokens they already lost.

Campaign 1 is still a real draft. There are no enablers. You must include the US F-16 card or the PRC J-10 card. You still take a normal Standard roster around it: 3 or 4 squadrons, not that one card alone.
Check yourself

You pick the AEW squadron first. Can the opponent then take your F-22 so you cannot have both?

No. Their deck is a different 54 cards. Your F-22 is still in your deck. Draft order only changes the order you click, not what you are allowed to own.

Before the fight

Missions

Each side secretly picks a mission. It defines what is worth victory points. You do not show it until the game ends, so you are always guessing why the opponent is saving a bomber or ignoring a damaged base.

Campaigns 1 and 2 lock both sides to Attrition. Longer campaigns let you choose.

Check yourself

You are on Attrition and you destroy an enemy AEW token and the squadron card it came from. How many points from those two?

5. The AEW token is worth 3. The squadron card is worth 2. They are scored separately.

Before the fight

Initiative and intel

After the draft, both sides roll a D4 to bid for initiative. You may sacrifice enabler cards for +1 each, except in the tournament campaign, where sacrifices are not allowed. High roll wins. Ties are handled by the app's roll.

The winner gets three things:

  • Intel advantage for the peek that follows, and the intel track flips to Advantage. It stays that way until a later cycle changes it.
  • The choice of who takes the first turn of this ATO.
  • One cyber raise attempt, a D4 against the access number for the next rate.

Then both sides play intel. Each rolls to see some cards in the opponent's hand. The initiative winner rolls that peek at advantage. At least one enemy card always stays hidden, so you never get a complete read.

Campaign 2 does this only once. You bid in ATO 1, with no card sacrifices. ATO 2 initiative goes to the side that did not hold it in ATO 1. It alternates. You do not roll again.
Check yourself

You win the initiative roll. What do you receive besides going into the turn order?

Intel advantage on the peek, and one attempt to raise your cyber rate. You also choose which side takes the first turn. That can be your opponent, if you want them to spend a squadron first.

Before the fight

Cyber

Each side starts at Cyber Rate 1. The track runs from 0 to 4. Raising it is a D4 against an access number that gets harder as you climb. Select your current rate.

Cyber cards and the initiative bonus are how you get those rolls. Many effects scale with the rate you already have:

  • Cyber Reconnaissance acquires enemy tokens equal to your rate.
  • Offensive Cyber removes enemy tokens equal to your rate, or forces that many enabler discards.
  • Joint Offensive Cyber removes tokens equal to twice your rate.
  • Cyber Counter-UAS destroys enemy drones equal to your rate.

The defender can degrade your rate with Defensive Cyber (down 1) or Joint Defensive Cyber (down 2), or cancel the card outright as a response.

Rate 4 wins the game immediately. It does not wait for the end of the ATO, and victory points are not compared. If a mission pays you for a high cyber rate, rate 3 is the highest you can sit on and still be playing for points.
Check yourself

You are at rate 2. What must you roll to reach 3, and what happens if you later reach 4?

You need a 4 on the D4 to go from 2 to 3. Reaching 4 ends the game. You win.

The turn

Your turn

On your turn you may do both of these, in either order, or you may pass.

  1. Play one enabler from your hand. Responses are separate: those fire on the opponent's turn when their trigger happens.
  2. Then exactly one of: activate a squadron, or a move-acquire-shoot action.

Activating a squadron ends the turn. You cannot launch aircraft and also move or shoot with pieces already in the air. Move, acquire, and shoot may come in any order. Each may be used once. You may skip any of them. Each one may use a different token.

The button says "Pass / end turn." That ends your turn, not the cycle. The cycle ends only when the opponent passes too. Playing an enabler and nothing else still counts as doing something, so the cycle continues.
Check yourself

Can you shoot with a fighter, then move that fighter, then acquire with your AEW, and also activate a second squadron?

The shoot, the move, and the acquire are legal together, in that order, because each action is used once and they may use different tokens. Activating a squadron on the same turn is not legal. Launching is instead of move-acquire-shoot, not in addition to it.

The turn

Activating a squadron

Activation flips a face-down squadron card face-up and creates its tokens. Where they appear depends on the type:

  • Fighters appear on your front band. US Band A. PRC Band E.
  • Drones may appear on any of the five center bands.
  • Bombers and AEW appear on the front band or in your standoff.
  • ADA appears on the airbase or CL where that card was deployed.
  • Carrier J-15s, from the Shandong enabler, may appear on any band.

A squadron only re-fields what it has left. If two of four F-22s were destroyed last cycle, the card launches two, not four. Destroyed tokens do not grow back unless a specific enabler says so.

Risky launches

  • From the US CL, a multi-token squadron rolls a D4 and launches only that many. ACE or Flying Crew Chief skips the roll.
  • A recovered squadron brought back by Rapid Resupply is marked recovered. When you activate it, roll a D4. A 1 breaks every aircraft on the ground and hands them to the enemy. A 2, 3, or 4 launches normally.
  • A Winchester fighter relaunch is its own turn, covered in the Winchester lesson. It also ends the turn.
Check yourself

Your B-52 card lost nothing, and you activate it. Where may the token appear?

On your front band, or in your standoff. One token. Activating it spends the turn, so the B-52 cannot bomb until a later turn.

The turn

Movement

You may move one token per turn, up to its move range in steps on the map. Fighters move 2. Bombers, AEW, drones, the EC-130, and ships move 1. ADA moves 0.

  • A grounded token cannot take a voluntary move. A Winchester token cannot either. Winchester aircraft go home at the start of your turn, and that trip is free.
  • Ships move among the five center bands only. They do not enter a base or a standoff.
  • Only bombers and AEW may move into standoff.
  • You cannot voluntarily move onto the contingency location. That box is for deployment and for Winchester recovery.
  • Moving one of your aircraft back onto its own base lands it for the rest of the ATO. It re-arms next cycle. If that squadron card is already destroyed, the token is lost.
  • Placing a token with an enabler is not your move action. It is part of the card.
A fighter's real reach. An F-22 on Band A can step to Band B or Band C. It can also step back toward its airbase. It cannot enter PRC standoff in one move, and it cannot carry a second fighter along. The second fighter waits for another turn.
Check yourself

Your attack drone is Winchester. Can you spend your move to send it home?

You do not need to. At the start of your turn it returns to its squadron card for free, if that card is still alive. A voluntary move is for a token that still has ammo and is not grounded.

Combat

Acquire and fog of war

Enemy tokens enter the board face-down. You can see that something is there, and you can see the number you must roll to identify it. You cannot see the type until you acquire it. Your own tokens are always face-up to you.

To acquire, pick one of your tokens that has an acquire range, pick one enemy token inside that range, and roll. Success is the kept die, plus any acquire bonus, greater than or equal to the target's AV. On a success the token flips face-up for the rest of the ATO.

  • AEW and recon drones roll twice on one attempt and take a success if either die works. AEW also has +2 and range 3.
  • Grounded aircraft cannot be acquired. They die with their squadron card.
  • ADA on a base can be acquired. Until it is, a base strike cannot assign damage to it.
  • You must acquire a ship, a standoff aircraft, or an ADA before a surface shot at that token. A strike on the base itself does not need a prior acquire.
  • Some cards acquire directly: cyber reconnaissance, space reconnaissance, SOF reconnaissance, ground-based radar.
Face-down in the app. A legal acquire is labeled with a question mark, an id, and the AV, such as Acquire ?#12 (av 4). The name stays hidden so the choice list does not leak it. After a hit, the log tells you what it was.
Check yourself

A face-down token on the enemy airbase is a grounded fighter. Can you acquire it and shoot it in the air?

No. Grounded aircraft cannot be acquired, and they cannot be shot as aircraft. You damage the squadron card with a base strike. If the card takes 2 hits, the grounded fighters die with it.

Combat

Shooting

You get one shot per turn. There are three kinds.

Air-to-air

An air token with an air attack fires at an acquired enemy aircraft in range, including one loitering in standoff. Standoff is one step past the front band, so a range-1 fighter must be in that front band to shoot into standoff. Roll greater than or equal to your air threshold. A hit destroys the token. There is no damage die. You cannot shoot a ship or an ADA this way.

Surface against a token

A token with a surface attack fires at an acquired ship, standoff aircraft, or ADA in range. A ship or an ADA dies in one hit. A hit on a standoff aircraft is applied to its squadron card: the card has 2 hits, and destroying the card destroys what is on it.

Base strike

Fire at an enemy airbase or CL in surface range. You do not acquire the base first. On a hit, an exploding attacker (B-52, H-6K, the missile ships, the flotilla) rolls a D4 for damage. Everyone else deals 1. You then assign those hits. The next lesson is that assignment.

Ammo cares about the hit die only. The damage die never makes you Winchester. If Forward Observers makes the strike an auto-hit, there is no hit die, and that counts as a clean 4: you do not go Winchester, and a ship spends no salvo.
Check yourself

Your F-35 hits an acquired J-16 in air-to-air range. Do you roll a second die for damage?

No. Air-to-air is a single-hit kill. The to-hit roll is the whole attack. A second die appears on exploding surface and base attacks.

Check yourself

You deal 1 damage to an enemy squadron card this cycle and do not finish it. What is that card's damage at the start of the next ATO?

Zero. Partial card damage resets every cycle. Only a second hit, which destroys the card, is permanent. Airbase VP boxes do not reset.

Combat

Winchester

Winchester means out of ammunition. It is decided by the natural to-hit roll, before bonuses, and never by the damage roll.

  • Most aircraft go Winchester on a 1, 2, or 3. A natural 4 means they still have weapons.
  • Attack drones go Winchester on a 1 or 2. A 3 or 4 keeps them armed.
  • ADA, AEW, recon drones, and the EC-130 never go Winchester.
  • Ships use salvos instead. They have 4 air salvos and 1 surface salvo. A natural 4 spends nothing. The ship is Winchester only when both magazines are empty. It can provide missile defense only while an air salvo remains.

At the start of your turn, your Winchester aircraft return to their squadron card. They are grounded and re-arm next cycle. The flight home is free. If the card was destroyed, they are surrendered to the enemy. Winchester ships leave the board. They are out of play, and they are not counted as destroyed.

Fighter relaunch

When a fighter comes home Winchester and the squadron card is still alive, you may spend a later turn to relaunch it. Roll a D4. A 1 breaks it on the ground and gives it to the enemy. A 2, 3, or 4 sends it back out as a fresh sortie on your front band, re-armed, and the enemy must acquire it again. A relaunch ends the turn, the way an activation does.

Check yourself

Your B-52 rolls a natural 2 to hit an airbase, then a 4 on the exploding damage die. Is it Winchester?

Yes. The hit roll was a 2, which is under 4. The damage die does not put the ammo back. It flies home at the start of your next turn.

Combat

Missile defense

If an attack's path crosses an air-defense weapon zone, the defender can make that attack harder.

  • ADA imposes disadvantage automatically. The defender does not spend an action and does not have to announce it.
  • A ship may be declared as the defender if the shot crosses its zone and it still has an air salvo. Declaring spends that salvo. It has to be declared before the attacker rolls. The app asks when it is time.
  • This covers air-to-air shots as well as surface and base strikes.
  • The disadvantage applies to the hit roll and, when there is one, the damage roll.

Some attacks ignore it. PRC hypersonic missiles, the maritime strike cruise missile, Sea Dragons, and PLANMC are unblockable. Air Launched Decoy and Decoy Warheads can cancel a missile-defense attempt that was declared.

A long PRC ADA. Long-range ADA shoots air out to 3 bands and defends shots that cross its zone. It is the piece that makes a casual bomb run expensive. Kill it with a surface attack after you acquire it, or spend a base-strike point on it once it is face-up.
Check yourself

Your shot crosses an enemy ship's zone. Does the disadvantage happen by itself?

No. ADA is automatic. A ship must be declared, and the declaration spends one air salvo, before you roll. If the ship has no air salvos left, it cannot defend.

Combat

Enablers during play

You may play one enabler on your own turn, before or after the activate-or-shoot half. Responses appear when their trigger fires, including on the opponent's turn. An enabler strike rolls a plain damage die. Advantage on that card changes the gate roll, not the damage.

Think of the hand in families:

  • Find. SOF, space, cyber, and radar cards acquire tokens without flying a sensor into range.
  • Remove. Offensive cyber deletes tokens or forces discards. Counter-UAS deletes drones. Submarines and the Marine Littoral Regiment kill a ship.
  • Strike the base. HIMARS and Tomahawk fill VP boxes. PRC ballistic, cruise, and hypersonic missiles hurt cards and boxes. Sea Dragons and PLANMC fill VP boxes and cannot be intercepted.
  • Put a unit on the map. Ships, the flotilla, the EC-130, land-based ADA, the Shandong's J-15s.
  • Bias the whole cycle. Improved Munitions, offensive and defensive EW, EW spoofing, space-based EW, Badger Surge, munitions upgrade.
  • Answer a disaster. Personnel Recovery and Quick-Turn bring a just-lost aircraft back, face-down. Red Horse and Resilient Bases cancel a base attack. Reserves regenerates a wiped squadron. Defensive cyber and counter-space cancel the matching enemy card.
  • Stretch the force. Aerial Refueling adds a squadron beyond the posture. Rapid Resupply returns a discarded card. Flying Crew Chief makes one CL squadron launch at full strength.

Recovered aircraft, a regenerated squadron, a Rapid Resupply revival, and a fighter relaunch all come back face-down. The enemy acquires them again. A cancelled shot is different: it is as if the shot never happened, so an earlier acquisition stays.

Check yourself

The opponent just destroyed your F-35 in the air, and you hold Personnel Recovery. When can you play it, and how does the F-35 come back?

As a response to that loss, which can be on their turn. It returns to your Band 1, face-down, and it is not scored as destroyed. They have to acquire it again.

Winning

Keeping score

Victory points come from your mission, plus one point per enemy airbase box you have filled. The app keeps the running total. You will not see the opponent's mission math until the end, except for points that are public, such as a base box.

When points are awarded depends on the mission:

  • Attrition, Interdiction, and Counter-Intervention read the destruction log. Kills count when they happen, and the mission table says how much each kind is worth.
  • Economy of Force, N-K Dominance, and Three Dominances score at the end of each ATO, from what you fielded, played, or still have on the tracks.
  • Enforce Rule of Law scores on the turn you activate a squadron or an enabler creates a token.
  • Airbase boxes score immediately, once each, for whoever filled them.
An Attrition tally. You kill a J-10 token (+1), sink a ship (+3), and destroy the J-20 squadron card (+2). You have also filled one PRC airbase box (+1). That is 7 VP from those events. The J-20's individual lost tokens, if you killed them in the air, would add +1 each on top of the card.

Campaign 4 adds +1 per enemy squadron wiped out entirely in the air, up to +2 per ATO. Campaign 5 adds +1 per intact squadron card at the very end.

Check yourself

You filled two airbase VP boxes in ATO 1. Do they score again in ATO 2?

No. Each box scores once, and the damage stays. You would need the third box for another point.

Winning

How you win

At the end of the last ATO, missions are revealed and the totals are compared. The higher total wins.

That comparison never happens if someone reaches Cyber Rate 4. The game stops. That side wins, whatever the VP track says.

There is no third sudden-death for destroying every squadron or filling every base box. A wrecked airbase still functions. A side with no aircraft left can still be ahead on points, or can still be holding a cyber win.

Play the mission you actually hold. On Economy of Force, an unused squadron is worth 2 at the end of the cycle. Activating it because it "should fight" can be the point you needed. On Attrition, that same quiet card is worth nothing until it kills something, or until the enemy kills the card.
Check yourself

You are behind 14 to 6 on victory points, and your cyber raise just moved you from 3 to 4. Who wins?

You do. Cyber Rate 4 ends the game immediately. The point totals are not compared.

Winning

Between ATO cycles

When both sides have passed, the cycle ends in a fixed order. Aircraft still in the air land on their cards. Landing on a destroyed card loses the token. End-of-ATO mission points are scored. Then the board clears.

What carries into the next cycle:

  • Destroyed tokens stay destroyed, and the squadron re-fields fewer.
  • A destroyed squadron card stays out and has already scored for the enemy.
  • One point of damage on a card that survived is erased.
  • Airbase VP boxes stay filled.
  • Cyber rate, intel, and victory points stay where they are.
  • You draft posture, squadrons, and enablers again from what you have left.

Played single-use enablers are gone. Unplayed single-use enablers, and every multi-use enabler, go back to your pool. Enduring biases such as electronic warfare expire. They were for that cycle only.

Check yourself

Your cyber rate is 3 and you have 8 VP at cleanup. What are they at the start of the next ATO, before the new initiative roll?

Still 3, and still 8. Both persist. The initiative winner may then get another chance to roll the rate upward.

Play

Using this app

The board is the map. Your legal choices are the buttons on the side. You never type a move. If a button is missing, the action is illegal right now: out of range, already used this turn, or hidden by fog.

  • Roll appears when a die is yours. Click it. The counted face stays bright.
  • The event log is the history. Dice, kills, and victory-point changes are marked.
  • Copy state for AI copies a text picture of what you are allowed to know, plus numbered legal actions. Paste it into a coach if you want advice. It does not reveal unacquired enemy types.
  • In two players, one device, the app hides the hand between turns. Hand the device over only after the handoff screen.
  • In two players, network, each device sees only its own side. The host shares the game code.
  • Practice vs Agent lets you play one side. Easy, Medium, and Best are the trained opponents. Random is useful while you are still learning the buttons. GPT needs your own API key and is optional.

A first game

  1. Go back to the menu. Choose Practice vs Agent.
  2. Campaign 1. Play as US. Agent AI — Easy or Random. Turn time and ATO time Off. Start.
  3. Posture will be Standard. Draft 3 or 4 squadrons and include the F-16. A useful learning set is F-16, F-15E (it can bomb), AEW (it can find), and F-22 (it is hard to see).
  4. Place them on the airbase. There is no enabler step.
  5. Roll initiative. If you win, take the first turn or give it away.
  6. Activate the AEW into standoff, or activate a fighter squadron onto Band A. That spends the turn.
  7. On later turns, acquire with the AEW, then shoot with a fighter that is in range. Move a fighter forward when the target is one band too far.
  8. When you have nothing you want to do, pass. If the opponent also passes, the cycle ends and you see the score.
Read the button, then the board. The choice text names the token id and the destination. Match the id to the piece on the map before you click. A face-down target stays a question mark until the acquire succeeds.
Check yourself

You already moved a fighter this turn, and the activate buttons disappeared. Can you still launch a squadron before the turn ends?

No. The move started move-acquire-shoot, and activation is no longer offered. You can still acquire and shoot if you have not used those. Launch the other squadron on a later turn. At the start of a fresh turn, activate and the maneuver buttons are both listed until you pick one branch.

Play

Easy to miss

These are the rulings that change a plan. They are all in force in the app.

  • Move, acquire, and shoot can happen in any order, once each, with different tokens.
  • Decks are separate. You draft your whole roster, then the opponent drafts theirs.
  • Ships move, one band at a time, on the five center bands.
  • Air-to-air does not kill ADA. A surface shot does, after you acquire it. A base strike can also hit an ADA that is already acquired.
  • A base strike does not pick off grounded fighters one by one. Those hits go on the squadron card. Two hits destroy the card and the aircraft on it.
  • Standoff aircraft can be shot in the air if acquired and in range. A range-1 fighter has to be in the adjacent front band.
  • Partial damage on a squadron card resets next cycle. Destroyed cards and filled VP boxes do not.
  • VP boxes do not close the airbase.
  • The CL has no VP boxes, and one strike there damages only one card.
  • Advantage and disadvantage cancel. They do not stack. Only missile defense puts disadvantage on the damage die.
  • A natural 4 on the attack roll keeps most aircraft armed. The damage roll cannot save them or disarm them.
  • An auto-hit from Forward Observers does not cause Winchester.
  • Coming back from recovery, reserves, or a relaunch is face-down. A cancel restores the old state, including an old acquisition.
  • Both players must pass in a row to end the ATO.
  • Cyber Rate 4 wins at once. You start at rate 1, not 0.

That is the whole game. The next useful hour is the Tutorial against Easy, with the clocks off. It is Campaign 2, so the draft includes the 6 enabler cards and the fight runs two ATO cycles.

Check yourself

Name the one track that can end the game before the victory points are compared.

Cyber rate. At 4, the game is over.
